## Section A: Overview

### Who we are

The DIV Fund is a tiered, evidence-based, open innovation fund that identifies and supports solutions with the potential to cost-effectively improve the lives of millions of people living in poverty around the world. We aim to serve as a discovery engine for global development, building a pipeline of evidence-driven innovations that drive impact at scale.

### How we fund

Our goal is to maximize the expected social return on our investment. To enable this, we support innovators and researchers through three funding tiers, calibrating funding according to our three core principles: evidence of impact, cost-effectiveness, and potential for durable scale. At earlier stages of funding, we look for potential. At higher funding levels, we look for demonstrated results. This tiered approach balances measured risk-taking with progressively higher requirements, in order to discover new ideas, validate what works, and catalyze the scale-up of proven solutions.

### What we fund

The DIV Fund supports innovators and researchers piloting, rigorously testing, and scaling ways to meaningfully and measurably improve the lives of people living in poverty. We are deliberately open to all kinds of innovations, operating across all sectors, with potential to impact the lives of millions of people when delivered at scale. We are open to innovations with any viable pathway to scale, be that through public systems, philanthropic support, private markets, or a combination of funding sources.

### Where we fund

The DIV Fund supports innovations addressing challenges in low- and middle-income countries (as classified by the World Bank). We prioritize proposals based on their expected social return, and give more weight to improvements expected to benefit people with lower incomes.

### What we mean by "innovation"

We take a broad view of innovation, considering any solution with real potential to generate impact at scale more affordably or more efficiently than existing approaches. Innovation is not limited to physical inventions. A relevant innovation could be a product, service, policy, program, or delivery model. An innovation might be an entirely novel idea, but it could also be a new way of delivering an existing solution, or a simple process improvement that unlocks impact. We also invest in evidence generation for widely-used approaches that don't yet have rigorous evidence.

### Who is eligible to apply

We accept applications from non-profit organizations, social enterprises, universities and research institutions, technical assistance providers supporting government innovation, and businesses, as well as partnerships among multiple types of organizations. We are unable to support individuals.

## Section B: Core Principles

Our review process is guided by three core principles: (1) evidence of impact on the lives of people living in poverty; (2) cost-effectiveness in improving development outcomes; and (3) potential for durable scale.

### 1. Evidence of impact

We rely on evidence of impact to guide our funding decisions, and at earlier stages, we help grantees build it. All applicants must present a credible theory of change grounded in existing evidence. We ultimately value outcomes with intrinsic value such as improved health, higher earnings, or academic achievement. We also accept evidence that captures intermediate outcomes for which rigorous evidence of impact on these end outcomes already exists.

By rigorous evidence, we mean causal measurement of impact — demonstrating that an innovation caused measurable improvements in people's lives, not merely correlation.

### 2. Cost-effectiveness

We look for innovations with the potential to deliver greater impact per dollar than the status quo or viable alternatives. Cost-effectiveness is a function of both cost and impact and does not simply mean that an innovation is the cheaper solution. We carefully consider current costs as well as projected costs at greater scale. We expect applicants to be clear about both the assumptions underlying their cost projections and the evidence behind their impact estimates.

### 3. Scale

We fund innovations with the potential to generate impact at scale, and that have a clear path to lasting financial sustainability. For DIV, "at scale" typically means improving the lives of at least one million people over ten years. However, in especially compelling cases, we may consider innovations that generate an exceptionally large benefit for a slightly smaller population.

We assess proposals distinctly according to their anticipated pathway to scale:

- **Public pathway:** If the innovation is designed to scale with government, donor, or philanthropic funding.
- **Commercial pathway:** If the innovation is designed to scale commercially (e.g., customer payments or advertising revenue).
- **Hybrid pathway:** If the innovation is designed to scale with a combination of commercial revenue and public or philanthropic funding.

Across all stages, we prioritize investments that maximize expected social returns. Since the number of people ultimately reached varies dramatically, scaling potential is weighted heavily in our analysis.

## Section C: Criteria by Stage

### Stage 1: Piloting — Up to $200K

**Purpose:** Stage 1 grants support real-world pilots of early-stage innovations. Stage 1 innovations are typically early in their development and use DIV funding for pilot testing to refine the basic model and establish real-world viability at a relatively small scale.

**Important:** The DIV Fund only supports innovations that are **post-prototype and ready to be tested in a real-world setting**. If your innovation is still in development in a lab or is still at the idea stage, it is premature for DIV support. We typically support pilots that are being tested with at least hundreds or thousands of people.

**Examples of supported activities:** Assessment of demand and willingness to pay, exploration of different delivery models, conducting user or market testing, and documenting social outcomes and real-world implementation costs.

**By the end of a Stage 1 award, we want to know:** How the innovation functions in practice, if people will use it, and if it shows enough early promise to warrant deeper investment.

#### Expectations for Stage 1 applicants:

**1. Evidence of impact:** We do not require causal evidence of impact at Stage 1, but we do expect a strong theory of change — your roadmap for how activities lead to real-world change — and a compelling rationale for how and why the innovation is likely to generate a positive development impact, relative to the status quo. Applicants should describe how they will collect, analyze, and use data during a grant to test the foundational assumptions in the theory of change.

- Public/philanthropic pathway: Include plans to pilot so that the innovation will be ready for rigorous testing of causal impact and cost-effectiveness by the end of the award period.
- Commercial pathway: Include plans to assess potential customers' demand and willingness to pay, or to conduct initial market testing.
- Hybrid pathway: Follow criteria for public pathway for the portion of costs expected to be covered by public or philanthropic subsidy, and commercial pathway for the portion covered by user fees or other private funding.

**2. Cost-effectiveness:** Clearly articulate current costs as well as how those costs will change as the innovation reaches greater scale. Define the level of scale used for these projections and explain the assumptions behind them. Provide a clear, data-informed case for why the innovation is likely to be cost-effective at-scale. Compare expected costs and outcomes to existing alternatives and identify key cost drivers.

**3. Scale:** Demonstrate that the innovation has the potential to reach millions of people. Describe how the innovation will be paid for at scale and make the case that there is potential for someone to cover these costs. Describe the types of partnerships needed and identify who is likely to finance, adopt, or sustain it.

### Stage 2: Testing & Positioning for Scale — Up to $500K

**Purpose:** Stage 2 grants determine if an innovation measurably and meaningfully improves people's lives and has a viable path toward scale. Applicants should have already conducted successful pilot testing and be ready to rigorously test their innovation's impact or market viability.

#### Expectations (summary):

**Evidence of impact:**
- **Public pathway:** If you don't yet have rigorous causal evidence, you'll need to generate it as part of the award. Initial application requires basic study design details (treatment/comparison groups, unit of assignment, power calculations).
- **Commercial pathway:** No causal evidence required but must show a credible theory of change. Must demonstrate a compelling case of market viability.
- **Hybrid pathway:** Show that any subsidies required are justified by the social value they create.

**Cost-effectiveness:** Demonstrate existing analysis or present a credible plan to analyze cost-effectiveness during the award.

**Scale:** Describe financial resources needed. Proposals are strongest when partnerships for scaling are already established.

---

### Stage 3: Transitioning to Scale — Up to $1.5M

**Purpose:** Stage 3 grants support the transition of proven innovations from piloting and testing to widespread scale. Applicants should have satisfied criteria for Stage 2 and be ready to address operational challenges of scaling.

#### Expectations (summary):

- **Public pathway:** Must demonstrate existing rigorous causal evidence of impact and a compelling cost-effectiveness case prior to application.
- **Commercial pathway:** Must have already demonstrated market viability consistent with Stage 2 criteria.
- **Hybrid pathway:** A combination of both.

---

## Key Program Details

- **Award periods:** Up to a maximum of five years
- **Co-funding:** Valued but not required
- **Catalytic role:** Grantees should achieve results that would not have been achievable without the grant
- **Social return focus:** The combination of per-person net impact, potential for durable scale, and DIV Fund's catalytic role drives assessment
- **Poverty weighting:** Higher weight given to a given dollar gain for poorer people
