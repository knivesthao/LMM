# 8. Sustainability Plan — Beyond the MegaGrant

Epic funds the spark. This section describes the fire.

The MegaGrant covers the 6-month build window. After month 6, LMM transitions from a funded project to a self-sustaining platform. We are not building something that requires indefinite grant dependency. LMM is designed from day one with a clear path to stand on its own.

---

## Revenue Model: The Creator Economy

LMM generates revenue through a marketplace model that aligns our interests with our creators:

- **Creator Subscription Tiers.** Publishers pay a monthly subscription to access the LMM creator platform. Tiers are differentiated by AI generation volume — controlling our API costs while letting professional creators scale.
- **Content Sales Split.** When a user purchases a comic or book from the digital library, the creator sets the price and receives 70% of the sale. LMM retains 30%.
- **Free Tier.** Casual creators (teachers, students) can create and publish for free with capped generation limits. This builds the library and the user base while creating a pipeline of creators who upgrade when they outgrow the free tier.

The unit economics at launch:
- A Lao comic typically sells for 10,000-20,000 kip ($0.50-$1.00) — accessible pricing for the target market
- With 100 creators and 500 active users at launch, revenue is modest but real
- The cost structure is dramatically lower than traditional publishing: no printing, no warehousing, no shipping, no returns

**Break-even target: 18 months post-launch** (August 2028), assuming 500+ paying users and 50+ subscription-tier creators.

---

## Subsidized Pipeline: Expanding Access Beyond Purchasing Power

Laos has a severe affordability constraint. Many of our target users — rural students, ethnic minority children, families on subsistence incomes — cannot pay even $0.50 per book. LMM addresses this with a two-channel model:

**Channel 1: Direct consumer sales.** Urban users, middle-class families, and English learners purchase content directly through the digital library with QR payments.

**Channel 2: Institutional and donor-funded access.** Schools, NGOs, and development organizations purchase content in bulk or sponsor free access for specific populations. This is where the sustainability plan connects to the broader funding ecosystem.

## Operational Sustainability: ADMAIS Is Already Running

A critical advantage that most grant-funded projects lack: LMM is not a new organization. It is an initiative of ADMAIS, which already operates as a sustainable business on both sides of this project.

- **ADMAIS US** already generates revenue from custom software development, AI agent development, and digital growth consulting. LMM extends these capabilities into a new vertical — educational media — without requiring a new company structure.
- **ADMAIS Laos** already serves small business clients in Laos with digital marketing and AI services. LMM adds a content creation and distribution product line to an existing, revenue-generating operation.
- **Infrastructure is shared.** The cloud infrastructure, development tools, and operational systems built for LMM improve ADMAIS's ability to serve other clients — and vice versa. This is not a project that exists in isolation.

If grant funding is delayed or reduced, ADMAIS can sustain LMM at a reduced burn rate using existing revenue. The platform does not die if a single grant application fails.

## Growth Trajectory: 3-Year View

**Year 1 (2026-2027):** Grant-funded build + launch. MegaGrant covers development. Platform launches with 40 titles, 100 beta users, 5 active creators. ADMAIS Laos begins creator recruitment pipeline.

**Year 2 (2027-2028):** Hybrid funding. Creator subscriptions begin contributing revenue. ISIF Asia grant (if awarded) funds distribution expansion and Lao creator training. User base grows to 500+. Library exceeds 200 titles. Platform reaches breakeven on operating costs.

**Year 3 (2028-2029):** Self-sustaining. Revenue from content sales and creator subscriptions covers all operating costs. Grant funding becomes optional — used for expansion, not survival. Cambodia pilot launches. Mobile apps live on iOS, Android, and Chinese app stores.

## Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Creator adoption slower than projected | Medium | Free tier ensures zero barrier to entry. ADMAIS Laos actively recruits and trains creators. Initial content library (40 titles) provides launch momentum even with few external creators. |
| User willingness to pay is lower than expected | Medium | Institutional/donor channel (Channel 2) provides revenue floor. NGOs and schools have budgets for educational materials even when individual users do not. |
| Follow-on grants not awarded | Low-Medium | Multiple grant targets reduce single-grant dependency. ADMAIS existing revenue provides fallback. Platform can operate at reduced scope on organic revenue. |
| AI costs exceed budget | Low | Self-hosted models (Llama 3, SDXL) provide cost ceiling. Subscription tiers with generation caps control per-user costs. Free tier is rate-limited. |
| Technical issues delay launch | Low | 6-month timeline includes buffer. Agile development with monthly milestones ensures early warning of delays. MVP scope (Phase 1-3) is achievable regardless of later-phase complexity. |
